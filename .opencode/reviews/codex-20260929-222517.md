## Constructor and city — live deployment review

**This is a partial review of the live build.** Both supplied screen URLs landed on the title menu. I reached the constructor and city through New Driver → Build, inspected screenshots at native device resolution, and tested body, armour, weapon and name changes. Sustained city driving could not be validated reliably with the available controls.

The deployment served `index-bVh14wWE.js`. Its constructor and city differ from the build described in your brief; the served bundle confirms that it does not handle `?screen=`. These findings apply to that live version.

### 1. Constructor: clicking the required car-name field prevents typing

**Where:** First row, at the top of the left parts panel.

**What is wrong:** Clicking the name field drops keyboard focus onto the page. Typing then does nothing. I reproduced this with both an empty field and an existing name. Reaching the field with Tab after focus had moved outside the builder allowed typing.

**Why it matters:** A name is mandatory. The obvious interaction fails precisely where a new player must act before building their first car. The LEGALITY panel continues saying “car name is required,” without explaining why the field ignores them.

**What I would do — code change:** Prevent clicks inside the input from activating its containing row. The served code lets the click bubble to the row handler, which rebuilds the constructor and removes the focused input. Preserve the input element and its focus while updating selection and statistics.

### 2. City: the map does not identify the destinations a player must choose

**Where:** Buildings around the central map, especially the repeated grey roof squares along its sides and lower edge.

**What is wrong:** Several facilities have identical roof sprites, with no visible facility names or service symbols. On arrival, the player cannot distinguish a place to obtain work from a place to buy or repair a car. The top instruction says to “head into a building or the gate,” but does not identify those destinations.

I checked the served city renderer: it draws facility sprites without a label layer, and the city overlay provides the generic instruction rather than destination names. This absence claim is supported by the live frame and served code.

**Why it matters:** Choosing a service becomes exploration by trial and error. That creates friction in the central job → preparation → departure loop.

**What I would do — code change:** Add persistent, screen-space facility labels anchored to the entry positions: “Assembly,” “Repairs,” and the other actual service names. Mark the gate as “Highway.” Highlight the relevant entry position when approached. Fit the city more generously into the viewport so those labels have room: the map occupied approximately one quarter of the screenshot’s width. This needs no change to the deliberately desaturated building palette and no new building art.

### 3. Constructor: the build action is clipped at the bottom of the scrolling list

**Where:** Bottom of the left panel, immediately above the keyboard-instruction line.

**What is wrong:** In the initial desktop layout, the “Confirm / Build this vehicle” row is already partly cut off by the list’s lower boundary. Installing a weapon inserts Facing and Ammo rows and pushes the build action further below the visible area. The action works when activated; its presentation is the problem.

**Why it matters:** After making a legal design, a player must find the action that completes it. The screen gives substantially more visible space to parts than to that essential next step.

**What I would do — code change:** Put a persistent “Build vehicle — $[cost]” button beneath the scrolling parts list. Keep all ten indexed weapon rows. Show the blocking reason beside the button when the design cannot be built, and ensure keyboard selection scrolls its selected row into view.

### 4. Constructor: the available budget appears only after the player exceeds it

**Where:** Cost statistic at the top of the right panel, and LEGALITY beneath it.

**What is wrong:** The initial design shows a cost of **$940**, but the constructor does not show the driver’s **$2,000** budget or remaining cash. I changed a Compact design with front armour to include a Machine Gun; at **$2,150**, LEGALITY finally disclosed “only $2000 available.”

**Why it matters:** The player learns their spending limit by making an unaffordable selection. Comparing bodies, protection and equipment requires remembering a balance from another screen.

**What I would do — code change:** Display three adjacent values: **Budget $2,000 · Build $940 · Remaining $1,060**. Update them with every change. Keep the affordability violation, but use it to explain a visible negative balance rather than introduce the budget.

### Separate game-wide gaps

I did not establish a game-wide gap in this scoped pass. The deployment mismatch is a review and release issue: publish the intended build and expose a visible build identifier before treating these observations as findings against the newer schematic constructor or revised city.

City movement and building-entry testing remain incomplete. Browser automation rejected input actions under this session’s approval policy, and desktop controls did not provide a reliable sustained-driving test.

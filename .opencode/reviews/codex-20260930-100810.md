## Gameplay review

I inspected the live arena, road, and city, viewed **2400 × 2020 screenshots at device pixel ratio 2**, and exercised movement, firing, vehicle entry, building entry, and pause/resume. I also entered Division 5 through the city to inspect combat.

### 1. High impact — the car’s combat geometry is rotated 90° from its driving direction

**Screen and location:** Arena, around the player’s car in the center of the playfield.

**What is wrong:** With the car’s nose and gun barrel pointing right, firing its front-mounted Machine Gun sends the projectile upward. I reproduced this with a single shot: ammunition changed **20/20 → 19/20**, and the paused frame shows the projectile directly above the right-facing car.

During Division 5 contact, an opponent also visibly overlapped a substantial part of the player’s chassis. The source check explains the disagreement: driving and sprite orientation treat local **+X as forward**, while weapon directions, armour facings, and collision rectangles treat **+Y as forward**.

**Why it matters:** A player who points the nose at an enemy fires sideways. The same disagreement makes visible body positions unreliable for judging contact and which armour facing receives a hit. This undermines aiming, ramming, and armour placement.

**What I would do — code change:** Define one vehicle coordinate convention and use it throughout driving, combat, damage, AI bearing checks, and collision geometry. Keeping the existing driving convention means front is +X and the collider’s length follows +X. Verify front-mounted shots and frontal impacts at all four cardinal headings, including two cars touching nose-to-nose.

### 2. Lower impact — entering the car fails silently outside its interaction range

**Screen and location:** City, the **“G enter/exit car”** instruction at the top and the parked car near the player.

**What is wrong:** After walking away from the parked car, pressing G produces no response or explanation. The pedestrian remains on foot. I separately confirmed that G works beside the car. The handler silently discards the `outOfRange` result; entry requires being within **3 meters**.

**Why it matters:** The instruction presents G as an available action without explaining its proximity requirement. A new player cannot distinguish being too far away from a failed keypress or broken control.

**What I would do — code change:** When entry fails, display a brief message such as **“Move closer to your car to enter.”** Show **“G — enter car”** near the vehicle when entry becomes available, and change it to **“G — exit car”** while driving. Derive availability from the existing interaction-range check.

## Gap in the game as a whole

### 3. Content gap — the Federal Building’s ordinary service is still a placeholder

**Where observed:** City, the blue Jobs marker near the starting gate and its building panel.

Walking into the Federal Building opens **“Federal Building isn’t open for business yet — coming in a future phase.”** I confirmed that its ordinary panel uses the stub implementation.

This leaves an explicitly unfinished destination in the city’s service network. The “closed” approach label communicates that honestly; the gap is the service content itself.

**What I would do — code and authored content:** Give it a small reception menu showing relevant federal quest hand-ins, requirements, and completion status. Preserve the existing automatic quest-delivery path, which runs before the panel opens; this finding does **not** establish that federal quest delivery is missing.

I established no separate road-specific defect in this pass. I drove off the carriageway, saw the return-to-road indicator, and opened and resumed the trip menu. I did not complete a highway journey or assess its full encounter pacing.

REVIEW-VERDICT: 3

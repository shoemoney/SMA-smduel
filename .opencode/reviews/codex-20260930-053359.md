## Gameplay review — ranked by player impact

I reviewed the live deployment with seed `a11ce5ee` at **1200 × 1010 CSS pixels, DPR 2**, and inspected the full **2400 × 2020** screenshots. I drove all three screens, fired weapons, entered a Division 5 event through the city, and exercised the trip menu, Journal, and Fleet.

### 1. Arena — combat has no usable pause

**Where:** During an arena event; there is no pause control alongside the top-right **Leave Arena** button.

**What is wrong:** Escape does not pause combat. In Division 5, after pressing Escape, the car accelerated from **5 to 25 mph over two seconds**, and all three opponents changed position on the radar. P also opened no menu. The source confirms the event loop continues without a pause gate.

**Why it matters:** A player cannot stop to check controls or attend to an interruption without continuing to expose their car and driver to combat. Leaving the event ends the match, so it does not serve the same purpose.

**What I would do — code:** Give arena events an Escape/P pause menu with **Resume, Controls, and Withdraw**. Freeze driving, AI, projectiles, damage, cooldowns, and match resolution together. Add a persistent “Esc — pause” hint.

### 2. Road — the radar covers and intercepts the trip menu

**Where:** Bottom-left, after opening **Esc — trip menu**.

**What is wrong:** The radar draws over the menu’s header, all four option numbers, and the beginnings of the labels. This also blocks interaction: hit-testing the centre of each option number returned the radar face rather than the menu option.

At the reviewed viewport, the radar occupies **x 12–192, y 755.5–998** CSS pixels. The menu rows begin at **x 65**, placing their left portion underneath it.

**Why it matters:** The player loses the numbered shortcuts precisely when choosing between resuming, checking controls, saving, and abandoning. Clicking the covered portion does nothing useful. The uncovered right portions remain clickable, but that workaround is not apparent.

**What I would do — code/CSS:** Put the pause menu in a centred overlay above the entire HUD, with a backdrop that receives input. Keep keyboard focus within the menu while it is open. Verify that both the option numbers and labels receive clicks.

[Menu screenshot](/Users/shoemoney/Projects/smduel/.shots/review-20260930-road-menu-repro.png)

### 3. Road — the recovery arrow points sideways when the road is below you

**Where:** Top-centre, the amber **“◀ Road — … m”** indicator.

**What is wrong:** I drove north of the horizontal carriageway and stopped. The road’s delineator posts were visible along the bottom of the frame, but the indicator read **“◀ Road — 14 m.”** The road was below the car, not left of it.

The source explains the mismatch: `roadRecoveryArrow()` returns only `◀` or `▶` from the lateral offset’s sign. It does not project the direction toward the road into the camera’s coordinates.

**Why it matters:** This is the recovery instrument for a player who has lost sight of the highway. Following its arrow can leave them travelling parallel to the road instead of returning to it.

**What I would do — code:** Calculate the vector from the car to the nearest point on the carriageway, project it through the camera, and rotate an arrow to that screen direction. In this captured state it should point **down**. Replace the current sign-only test with checks that compare arrow direction against the projected road position.

### 4. City — Journal and Fleet are working features with undisclosed controls

**Where:** The top-centre instruction strip, which currently teaches movement and **G enter/exit car**.

**What is wrong:** **J opens Journal** and **F opens Fleet**, but neither shortcut appears in the city’s visible instructions or as a desktop action button. I opened both successfully; the features exist.

**Why it matters:** Players can miss the place that tracks their courier work and campaign leads, or the roster used to manage their cars. These are central parts of the city loop, yet discovering them currently requires prior knowledge or experimentation.

**What I would do — code/UI:** Add visible **Journal [J]** and **Fleet [F]** buttons, wired to the existing handlers. Include those shortcuts in the city’s control reminder. The Journal button can show an active-task count once work is accepted.

[Journal screenshot](/Users/shoemoney/Projects/smduel/.shots/review-20260930-city-journal.png)

### 5. Arena — the entry message instructs the player to find an exit gate that is not an exit mechanism

**Where:** Top-centre message feed when entering Division 5.

**What is wrong:** The message says **“drive out the gate to exit.”** In the running arena I encountered the unbounded field described in your decisions. Source confirmation shows that the event ends through **Leave Arena** or a terminal match condition; there is no driving-through-a-gate exit check.

**Why it matters:** A player trying to retreat receives an instruction that cannot accomplish the promised action, while opponents continue moving.

**What I would do — code/string:** Change the message to name the actual control: **“3 opponents — use Leave Arena to withdraw.”** This requires a wording correction and no new art. The unbounded arena decision can stand.

## A real gap in the game as a whole

**Highway abandonment has no playable survival phase.** I selected **Abandon trip** and returned directly to New York. The source contains an `abandonVehicle()` function that creates a pedestrian at the stranded car, but the application does not call it.

That leaves a gap between driving a car and surviving after leaving it. I would implement a short roadside escape sequence using the existing pedestrian movement: reach a rescue point, face a defined risk or time cost, and then return to a city with an explicit vehicle-recovery outcome. This is primarily a **gameplay code feature**, rather than a screen-polish task.

I did not establish full-route completion, highway combat balance, arena victory handling, or save restoration. The review above is limited to the interactions I exercised and the source checks used to resolve ambiguous claims.

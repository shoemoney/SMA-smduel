## Confirmed findings, ranked by player impact

**This review is incomplete.** I captured and inspected full-resolution screenshots of title, controls, driver, constructor, city and arena, and interacted with the constructor and drove in city and arena. Before I could complete road and fleet testing, the browser loaded a different bundle and the shared Chrome session began changing screens and car names between my actions. The findings below apply to the initially tested live build, `index-SZR-64oQ.js`.

### 1. The constructor tells players to use a key that does nothing

**Screen and location:** Constructor, the lower-right LEGALITY instructions and the empty weapon rows on the left.

**What is wrong:** The instruction says, “Mount at least one weapon — press Enter on a weapon row.” I selected Weapon 1 and pressed Enter: it remained empty. Pressing Right mounted a Machine Gun immediately. The source confirms that Enter only confirms a build when the Confirm row is selected.

**Why it matters:** This instruction sits directly in the guidance for making the first car road-legal. Following it leaves the player stuck, with no explanation of why the prescribed action failed.

**What I would do — code and copy change:** Change the instruction to “Select a weapon row, then use ←/→ to choose a weapon.” Show that hint beside the selected empty row as well. If Enter is intended to open a weapon picker, implement that behavior before advertising it.

### 2. Naming the car makes the road-readiness guidance disappear prematurely

**Screen and location:** Constructor, lower-right LEGALITY panel.

**What is wrong:** Initially, the panel tells the player to name the car, fit armour and mount a weapon to make it road-legal. After I entered a name, it changed to **“No violations — ready to build”**, while the preview still reported **0 armour · 0 mounted**. It also showed that success message after adding one armour point while leaving every weapon row empty.

The constructor is reporting whether the design can be constructed; the surrounding instructions present this as guidance for getting onto the road. The city separately identifies an unarmoured, unarmed car as “Not road-legal.”

**Why it matters:** The player loses the outstanding requirements at the moment they start following them. The green success message encourages them to spend money and leave the constructor before completing the preparation the game just requested.

**What I would do — code change:** Keep two explicit statuses:

- **Build valid:** physical limits, name and available cash.
- **Road ready:** the actual departure requirements, with completed and outstanding items shown individually.

Allow a valid unarmed build if that is intentional, but label its result “Can build; cannot depart yet.” Keep the armour and weapon checklist visible until those requirements are satisfied.

### 3. Browser shortcuts are interpreted as game input

**Screen and location:** Constructor, especially the selected Name row; also the focused Controls menu.

**What is wrong:** With the constructor focused, pressing Command+L failed to focus Chrome’s address bar and appended `l` to the car name. Repeating it appended another `l`. Both the builder and menu handlers call `preventDefault()` without first excluding browser shortcuts.

**Why it matters:** A player trying to navigate, copy, or leave the page can accidentally edit their build. It also makes the application feel trapped: ordinary browser behavior stops working while a game panel has focus.

**What I would do — code change:** Return immediately for events carrying `metaKey`, `ctrlKey` or `altKey`, except explicitly supported game combinations. Call `preventDefault()` only for keys the current panel actually handles.

### 4. The WEAPONS panel covers the Arcade navigation link

**Screen and location:** Arena, extreme top-left.

**What is wrong:** The persistent Arcade link and the WEAPONS panel occupy the same corner. In the inspected frame, the weapons panel covers almost the entire link; parts of its border remain visible behind the panel.

**Why it matters:** The navigation control becomes visually obscured, and two independently positioned interface elements look accidentally stacked. This is lower impact because Exit to Title remains visible at the top-right.

**What I would do — code/layout change:** Give navigation and HUD a shared layout rule. Reserve space above the weapons panel for the Arcade link, or place the link alongside Exit to Title in a single top navigation strip. No new art is needed.

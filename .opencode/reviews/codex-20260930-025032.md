## Gameplay review — ranked by player impact

I reviewed the live **arena, road, and city** with seed `a11ce5ee`, inspected full **2400 × 2020 screenshots at DPR 2**, and exercised movement on all three, arena firing, city car entry, and facility entry. Source checks below confirm behavior observed in the browser. Travel-duration estimates are calculated from the verified movement conversion; I did not complete a 150-mile trip.

### 1. Road — a routine journey takes roughly two hours of real driving

**Where:** The highway and the top-centre “En route to Albany · 150mi remaining” banner.

**What is wrong:** Route mileage advances at real-world speed. I reached **70 mph** while driving, but the odometer advanced only a few tenths of a mile and the destination still read **150mi remaining**. The movement code converts mph to metres per second normally, and route progress measures actual displacement along the route.

At a steady 70 mph, this trip takes **128.6 minutes**, before acceleration, stops, or fighting. Even a 90 mph car needs **100 minutes**.

**Why it matters:** This makes travel a major barrier to the city–job–delivery loop. The initially empty progress bar is correct; the problem is how much real time is required to fill it.

**What I would do — code change:** Separate strategic route mileage from local driving distance. Keep normal vehicle physics during encounters, but compress uneventful travel into short cruising intervals that advance route mileage and calendar time. Target approximately **3–5 minutes for this starter journey**, with encounters adding meaningful duration. Use the same strategic progress for arrival, encounter placement, job deadlines, and the progress bar.

### 2. Road — the player cannot deliberately pause or leave the trip

**Where:** The entire road screen; there is no trip-menu control alongside the HUD or objective.

**What is wrong:** I tried **Escape** and **P**. Neither opened a menu or paused the simulation; the car continued coasting. The road’s input handling supplies driving, weapons, and wreck searching, but no pause or trip-exit action. Practice arena, by comparison, has a visible “Exit to Title” button.

**Why it matters:** A player who chooses the wrong destination, wants to check controls, or needs to stop has to use browser navigation. This is especially damaging given the journey duration above.

**What I would do — code change:** Add a visible **Menu · Esc** control. Opening it should freeze driving, opponents, projectiles, cooldowns, and trip time. Provide **Resume**, **Controls**, and **Save and quit**. Save and restore the actual trip state so quitting does not restart the journey. An “Abandon trip” option should state its consequences before applying them.

This remains necessary even after travel is shortened.

### 3. City — facility identification appears when entry has already happened

**Where:** The facility markers around the plaza and the bottom-centre facility-name strip.

**What is wrong:** The name strip exists, including **“Federal Building — closed.”** However, approaching the lower-right blue briefcase marker took me straight into the Federal Building’s placeholder panel. The identifying strip appeared behind that panel.

The source explains why: the name becomes visible within **3 metres**, which is also the automatic doorway-entry radius. Entry is handled before the name strip updates. Consequently, this “approach” label normally identifies the building on the same frame that opens it.

**Why it matters:** The player cannot use the label to choose between facilities sharing a pictogram. Four yellow workshop markers and three blue briefcase markers are visible in this city. Discovering which one is useful requires entering and dismissing menus; discovering that one is unfinished happens after committing to it.

**What I would do — code change:** Give identification a larger radius than entry—approximately **6 metres** here—and place the nearest facility’s name beside its marker. At the entry radius, show **Enter [facility] · E**, requiring an explicit press. Mark unfinished facilities as closed before the player reaches them. This can retain the existing building palette and family pictograms.

### 4. Road — guardrails suggest a boundary that the car passes through

**Where:** The repeated steel rail segments along both highway shoulders.

**What is wrong:** From a fresh road start, I held **W for 2.2 seconds**. The car crossed the upper roadside rail and continued onto the surrounding field with unchanged condition. In a longer test, the highway disappeared completely from view.

The source explicitly treats these rails as decorative and provides no prop collision. That is an intentional implementation choice, but I disagree with the visual promise: a steel guardrail communicates a physical barrier.

**Why it matters:** Players make avoidance and escape decisions based on apparent solid objects. Passing through the rail breaks that expectation. Once the highway leaves the frame, the road screen also provides little help recovering its position.

**What I would do — new art plus code:** For the current free-driving model, replace rail segments with **roadside delineator posts and shoulder paint**, which communicate the route without implying a continuous barrier. Add an edge indicator showing the direction back to the carriageway whenever it is off-screen. If steel rails are retained, implement collision and feedback consistent with their appearance.

This finding concerns the highway furniture; the deliberately unbounded practice arena is fine.

### 5. Arena — ordinary firing fills the forward view with repetitive messages

**Where:** The top-centre message panel, directly above the player’s car.

**What is wrong:** Holding fire for **1.5 seconds** consumed five rounds and produced five separate **“Machine Gun fired”** messages. The feed grew into an approximately **260 × 178 CSS-pixel** panel with a conspicuous scrollbar. The old practice-entry announcement remained at the top, while the newest firing line fell below the visible area.

**Why it matters:** Routine firing creates a persistent obstruction in the direction the player is shooting. Identical shot announcements also compete with messages that deserve attention, such as damage or destruction.

**What I would do — code change:** Remove per-shot text from the visible feed; ammunition, cooldown, and firing effects already confirm the action. Reserve the feed for consequential events, show the newest two lines, and expire ordinary notices after a few seconds. Keep full history available through an expandable log.

## Real gap in the game as a whole

**Several advertised facility types still have no gameplay behind them.** I entered the Federal Building and received “isn’t open for business yet — coming in a future phase.” The facility dispatcher confirms that **hotel, federal, story, studio, and petshop** all use the placeholder engine.

That is a content-completion gap, separate from the city’s late identification defect. The practical cost is that travelling to a specialised destination can yield only a notice and a Leave option.

**What I would do — code and content:** Advertise functional destinations in the playable loop, and clearly mark remaining buildings as unavailable scenery. Prioritise completing facilities that support progression or give a city a distinctive reason to visit. New exterior art is not required to resolve this gap.
